# Design Principles at Focus Bear

## Research & Learn

**What are Focus Bear's design priorities? (Simplicity, low-friction interactions, accessibility)**
From using the app, it seems like the priorities are cutting down decisions, guiding people step by step instead of leaving them to figure it out, and keeping things calm rather than busy. Everything points toward less friction, a clear next step, simple routines instead of open-ended planning, and making sure the app itself doesn't turn into another distraction.

**How should UI elements, colors, and typography support neurodivergent users?**
Colors and text should support focus, not fight for attention, so calmer palettes and clear, readable text matter more than flashy visuals. Using the same color consistently, like always using one color for "in progress," helps people recognize meaning at a glance instead of reading everything closely. Text should stay simple and easy to read, without anything decorative adding extra noise.

**Why does consistency in navigation & interaction patterns matter for usability?**
If navigation changes or works differently across parts of the app, every screen has to be relearned, which adds mental effort. Keeping patterns consistent means once you learn one part of the app, you already know how the rest works too, so your attention goes toward the task instead of the interface.

**How can small UI details (e.g., button placement, animations, tooltips) improve or harm usability?**
Small details help a lot when they're predictable, like a button always being in the same spot, or a small animation confirming something happened. But they can also hurt usability fast if overused, too many animations or tooltips popping up can end up being the exact distraction the app is meant to prevent. The goal should always be less friction, not extra polish for its own sake.

I actually saw a good example of this while exploring the app itself. Focus Bear's compact menu bar widget has one big, obvious button, "Start Focus Session," with everything else underneath as simple rows with an icon and one line of text. There's no confusion about what to do first, even in a tiny popup, which is exactly the kind of small detail that cuts down decision fatigue instead of adding to it.

## Reflection

**How can Focus Bear maintain a minimal, distraction-free UI while still offering powerful features?**
By hiding complexity behind simple entry points instead of showing everything at once. A feature can still be powerful without being visible all the time, most people just need the next step shown clearly, with more advanced options there if they go looking, not forced onto the screen.

**If a design change improves aesthetics but reduces usability, how should UX handle it?**
Usability should win. A screen that looks nicer but is harder to use isn't actually a better design, it just looks better in a screenshot. I'd push back on the change, or find a version that keeps the visual improvement without making the actual task harder.

**What are the risks of overloading users with too many choices or settings?**
Too many choices means more decisions before the task even starts, which is exactly the friction Focus Bear is trying to remove. It also makes the app harder to trust, if there's a setting for everything, people start wondering if they're using it "right." Fewer, well-chosen defaults usually work better than exposing every option.
