# Agile at Focus Bear – Reflection

## Research & Learn

**What is Agile, and how does it differ from traditional project management?**  
Agile is a flexible way of working that focuses on quick iterations, constant feedback, and adapting as you go. Traditional project management is more rigid, with long planning phases and fixed timelines. Agile changes direction easily; traditional methods stick to the original plan.

**Core values and principles of Agile (Agile Manifesto)**  
Agile values people over processes, working software over documentation, collaboration over contracts, and responding to change over following a strict plan. The principles encourage continuous improvement, frequent delivery, open communication, and adapting to feedback.

**Differences between Scrum and Kanban**  
Scrum works in fixed sprints (usually 1–2 weeks) with roles like Scrum Master and Product Owner. It has ceremonies like stand‑ups, sprint planning, and retros.  
Kanban is more flexible — work flows continuously, tasks move across a board, and there are no fixed sprints or formal roles. You focus on limiting work‑in‑progress and improving flow.

**Why Focus Bear leans towards Kanban**  
Focus Bear prefers Kanban because the team is small, fast‑moving, and works on many different tasks. Kanban fits better when priorities shift often and you don’t need heavy ceremonies or strict sprint cycles.

## Reflection

**Biggest benefits and challenges of Agile**  
Benefits: faster feedback, easier collaboration, quick changes, and less pressure to “get everything perfect” upfront.  
Challenges: constant communication is needed, priorities can shift quickly, and you need discipline to stay organised.

**How Agile principles can help my role**  
Agile helps me stay flexible, communicate clearly, and break work into smaller steps. It encourages me to ask questions early, share updates often, and improve continuously.

## Task

**Main differences between Scrum and Kanban (in my own words)**  
Scrum uses sprints, roles, and structured meetings. Kanban is continuous, simple, and focuses on flow. Scrum is more formal; Kanban is more flexible.
