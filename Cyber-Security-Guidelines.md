# Cyber Security Guidelines

## Research & Learn

**What are common cyber security threats in a remote work environment?**  
Remote workers often face risks like phishing emails, weak passwords, unsafe Wi‑Fi networks, and malware. Because people work from different locations, attackers try to trick them into clicking harmful links or giving away login details. Even small mistakes can lead to big problems.

**What are best practices for keeping your devices and accounts secure?**  
Keeping software updated, using strong passwords, enabling 2FA, and avoiding unknown links all help protect your accounts. It’s also important to use secure Wi‑Fi, avoid sharing devices, and make sure your computer locks automatically. Small habits make a big difference.

**Why is it important to lock your computer when away from your desk?**  
Locking your computer prevents anyone from accessing your work or personal information. Even stepping away for a minute can be enough time for someone to see or change something they shouldn’t. Locking your screen is one of the simplest ways to stay secure.

**How should you handle phishing attempts and suspicious links?**  
If something looks off — strange sender, weird spelling, unexpected attachments — don’t click it. Report it, delete it, or check with your team first. Phishing works by catching people off guard, so slowing down and double‑checking helps avoid mistakes.

**What makes a strong password, and why should you use a password manager?**  
A strong password is long, unique, and hard to guess. Password managers help you create and store secure passwords without needing to remember them all. They also reduce the chance of reusing the same password across multiple accounts.

**Why is two-factor authentication (2FA) important, and when should you enable it?**  
2FA adds an extra layer of protection by requiring something you know (your password) and something you have (a code or device). Even if someone steals your password, they still can’t get in. You should enable 2FA on any account that supports it, especially work accounts.

## Reflection

**What security measures do you currently follow, and where can you improve?**  
I already use strong passwords and avoid suspicious links, but I can improve by checking that all my accounts have 2FA enabled. I can also make sure my devices lock automatically and review how I store passwords.

**How can you make secure behaviour a habit rather than an afterthought?**  
Keeping things simple helps — using a password manager, enabling auto‑lock, and updating software regularly. When security steps are built into your routine, they stop feeling like extra work.

**What steps will you take to ensure your passwords and accounts are secure?**  
I’ll use a password manager, turn on 2FA everywhere possible, and avoid reusing passwords. I’ll also check my accounts regularly to make sure nothing looks unusual.

**What would you do if you suspected a security breach or suspicious activity on your account?**  
I’d change my password immediately, enable 2FA if it wasn’t already on, and report the issue to the team. Acting quickly helps prevent further damage.

## New Cyber Security Habit  
I will make sure my computer and phone auto‑lock after a few minutes so my information stays protected even if I step away.
