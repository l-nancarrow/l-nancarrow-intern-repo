# Roles in an Agile Team

## Research & Learn

**What are the key roles in an Agile team, and what does each role do?**

Developers build the product, basically turning designs and requirements into actual working code. QA tests everything before it ships and catches the bugs nobody else noticed. The Product Manager decides what gets built and in what order, based on what users need and what matters for the business. The Agile PM keeps the process running, unblocking people, running the ceremonies, making sure work doesn't stall. Then there's a bunch of supporting roles, stakeholders who give feedback and sign off, UX designing the experience and doing research, and customer support who bring real user problems back to the team.

**How do these roles collaborate in Kanban vs. Scrum?**

Scrum runs on a set rhythm, sprint planning, standups, reviews, retros, so everyone has fixed points where they sync up, and roles like Scrum Master or Product Owner are pretty clearly defined. Kanban doesn't have that rhythm at all. It's more continuous, whoever's up next just pulls the next task off the board when they're ready instead of waiting for a sprint to start or end. The roles are still there, it's just a looser, more in-the-moment way of working together.

## Reflection

**What is your role, and how does it contribute to the Agile process?**

I'm in UX, so my job is making sure whatever gets built actually makes sense to the people using it. That's research, wireframes, prototypes, testing ideas out before they become real engineering work. I'm usually a step ahead of development, shaping the problem before it's ready to be picked up, but I also come back in during user testing to see if what got built actually holds up in practice.

**How do your responsibilities overlap or interact with other roles in the team?**

Mostly with the PM. We're both thinking about what the product should do and why, just from different angles, they're weighing priorities and business impact, I'm weighing usability. On the other side I overlap with developers, turning designs into something they can actually build, and answering questions when something doesn't quite make sense once it's in code.
