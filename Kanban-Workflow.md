# Agile Workflows & Kanban

## Research & Learn

**How does a Kanban board work, and how does it help manage workflow?**
It's basically a visual to-do list for a team. Every task is a card, and the card sits in whatever column matches its current status. Instead of asking around to find out what's going on, you look at the board.

**What do the different columns on a Kanban board represent (e.g. Backlog, In Progress, Blocked, Done)**
Each one is a stage a task passes through. For my board: Not started, In progress, Ready for review, Approved by bot. For a UX board it'd be more like To Do, In Design, User Testing, Approved, Live. Different projects, same idea, a column is a real state the work is in, not just a label.

**How do tasks move through the board, and who is responsible for updating them**
Usually whoever's actually doing the work moves their own card, since they're the one who knows it's actually done. The exception is anything automated, like a bot approving a PR, which updates on its own without anyone touching it.

**What are the benefits of limiting work in progress (WIP)**
It stops everyone from starting five things at once and finishing none of them. If "In progress" is capped and it's full, that's your sign to finish something before picking up anything new, instead of just quietly piling up half-done work.

## Reflection

**How does Kanban help manage priorities and avoid overload?**
You can literally see it if someone's overloaded, their column is stacked. That's a lot easier to catch than realising three weeks later that someone was drowning the whole time.

**How can you improve your workflow using Kanban principles?**
Honestly, just being better about updating the board in real time instead of at the end of the day. A board that's out of date is almost worse than no board. A WIP limit would help too, so things actually get finished instead of sitting half-done.
